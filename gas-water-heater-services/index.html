Most gas water heaters in Livonia, MI work the same basic way. Gas enters through a control valve that doubles as the thermostat and feeds a burner beneath the tank, and the hot exhaust travels up a flue through the middle of the tank and out through the vent. A thermocouple, or a flame sensor on newer electronic models, tells the valve a flame is present; without that signal, the valve closes for safety. The design is dependable, but it has a few predictable weak spots. Thermocouples wear out, pilot tubes and burner ports clog, the flame arrestor screen at the base packs up with lint and dust, control valves fail, and vents lose their draft.

Every gas water heater call starts with safety and then moves to testing. If there is a gas odor, leave the home first, call the gas utility from outside, and do not operate switches or try to relight anything. Once the area is safe, our Livonia technicians work through the heater systematically: millivolt readings on the thermocouple, a close look at the pilot and main burner flames, gas pressure at the control valve, a draft test to make sure exhaust is not spilling back, and a CO check around the appliance. Scorch marks at the burner access, rust flakes on the burner, and a lazy yellow flame each point to a different problem, and we chase down the right one.

The fix is usually straightforward once the cause is known. A new thermocouple or flame sensor, a cleaned pilot tube and burner, a cleared flame arrestor screen, or a replacement gas control valve gets most heaters running again the same day. Vent problems, like a disconnected connector or a flue pipe without enough rise, are corrected on the spot. If sediment is rumbling in the tank, we flush it, and if the anode rod is spent, we can replace it. When replacement is the smarter move, we install new gas heaters sized to your household's first-hour demand, with proper atmospheric, power vent, or direct vent venting, a drip leg on the gas supply, and the expansion tank, drain pan, and strapping your local code calls for. Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Exhaust odor, rust, or moisture around the draft hood and vent pipe
  • Less hot water than the tank should deliver
  • A pilot light that will not stay lit
  • Popping, rumbling, or crackling sounds as the burner heats
  • No hot water at all, with the burner never firing
  • A rotten-egg odor near the water heater, in which case get out and call the gas company first
  • A flame that looks orange or floppy rather than steady and blue
  • Water pooling around the base of the heater

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Have the anode rod inspected every few years and replaced when it is worn
  • Keep working CO alarms near bedrooms and on each floor
  • Check that the vent connector is secure and rising toward the chimney or flue
  • Never store flammable liquids near a gas water heater
  • Vacuum lint and dust from the flame arrestor screen at the base a few times a year
  • Flush the tank on a regular schedule so sediment never bakes onto the bottom
  • Set the gas valve near 120 degrees, because hotter water costs more and can scald
