How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Water at the tap that is suddenly scalding hot
  • Sudden loud knocking or boiling sounds coming from inside the tank
  • Scorch marks, soot, or flames rolling out near the burner access on a gas unit
  • The smell of gas near the unit, a situation that calls for evacuating and phoning the utility or 911
  • An electric water heater that keeps tripping its circuit breaker
  • Water pooling around the base of the heater or spreading across the floor
  • Every tap running cold and the heater showing no signs of life
  • A T&P valve that releases water repeatedly instead of staying closed

Act Before It Gets Worse

Left alone, even minor symptoms like rumbling or temperatures that swing from hot to warm tend to mean the heater is working its way toward a breakdown. If you notice any of these signs, contact Livonia Water Heater at (734) 280-6296 for a prompt inspection.

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

A water heater emergency packs several hazards into one closet or garage corner. There is water on the floor near a 240-volt circuit on electric models, or near a gas valve, burner, and flue on gas units. There is a tank holding very hot water under pressure. And there is a safety valve that, if it is discharging, has a reason for it. Homeowners sometimes try to stop the relief valve by capping it, tighten a leaking fitting until it cracks, or relight a pilot beside a gas smell, and each of those can turn a bad afternoon into something far worse. The safest moves for a homeowner are the ones described above: leave for gas, then shut off water and energy, then call for help.

Professional help is not just about having wrenches. It is about knowing why the failure happened so it does not repeat a few weeks later. When a relief valve discharges, we measure static water pressure and check whether an expansion tank is missing or waterlogged. When a tank floods the floor, we locate the true source, whether it is the valve, a fitting, a connector, or the tank wall. When a gas heater dies, we check thermocouple or flame sensor signal, gas pressure, and venting before restarting it. The diagnosis determines everything that follows. Components can be replaced the same visit; a failed tank cannot be patched, and we say so plainly and explain the replacement path.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Keep boxes, paint, gasoline, and cleaning products well away from a gas water heater
  • A drain pan with a proper drain line under the tank can turn a flood into a manageable puddle
  • Drain a few gallons or flush the tank annually to keep sediment from cooking the tank floor
  • Find and label the cold-water shutoff at the heater and the main house valve now, before you ever need them
  • A simple water sensor on the floor beside the tank can warn you hours earlier
  • A 120F setting is safer for skin and easier on the heater than the hottest setting
  • Check the temperature and pressure relief valve each year so you know it will work when it matters
