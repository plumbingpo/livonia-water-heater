If you could see inside your water heater, the case for maintenance would make itself. On the bottom there is a bed of mineral sediment, sometimes several inches deep, that makes the burner work harder, causes that familiar rumbling, and can burn out the lower element on an electric unit. Hanging from the top is the anode rod, meant to corrode first, which may be down to bare wire after a few years. When it is used up, corrosion goes after the tank lining, and a leak is only a matter of time. For homeowners in Livonia, MI, a yearly visit is the simplest way to keep both of those problems from shortening the life of the heater.

We work through a full checklist every time. The tank is drained and flushed to clear the sediment layer. The anode rod comes out for inspection and is replaced when little of it remains. The T&P relief valve is tested so we know it will open under pressure and close again cleanly. Gas heaters get a look at the burner, pilot assembly, thermocouple or flame sensor, flue, and draft. Electric heaters get element and thermostat testing and a check of the wiring connections. We also measure the air charge in the expansion tank, check the water pressure, set the thermostat to a scald-safe level unless a mixing valve allows higher storage, and inspect the drain valve, connections, and pan.

Tankless heaters skip the sediment bed but have a scale problem of their own. Minerals coat the heat exchanger over time, reducing efficiency and eventually setting off error codes. Our technicians connect a circulation pump to the isolation valves and run a descaling solution through the exchanger, clean the inlet screen, inspect the venting, and check the condensate drain on condensing models. Every visit ends with a clear report, noting what is healthy and what needs attention. Call (734) 280-6296 to book maintenance for your Livonia water heater.

Signs You Need Water Heater Maintenance in Livonia

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • Energy bills creeping up with no change in hot water use
  • More than a year since anyone flushed the tank
  • Rusty or discolored hot water at the tap
  • Hot water with a sulfur odor
  • A tank that pops and knocks during a heating cycle
  • A tankless unit showing error codes or fluctuating temperatures
  • Moisture, drips, or rust around the base or fittings
  • Less hot water available than when the tank was new

Don't Wait for a Total Failure

Tanks rarely fail all at once. They corrode from the inside for months, and the warning signs show up long before the floor gets wet. If you notice any of these signs, contact Livonia Water Heater at (734) 280-6296 for a prompt inspection.

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

Not every maintenance task is a good DIY project. Keeping clutter away from the heater, checking for leaks, and noticing new noises are all useful. Flushing, anode replacement, relief valve testing, and burner or element work are another story. Hot water, seized fittings, brittle drain valves, and sediment that clogs the valve midway through can turn a simple job into a flooded floor. Relief valves on older heaters sometimes fail to close after testing. And gas-side checks involve flame quality and venting, which protect your household's air.

How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Glance at the pan and connections regularly so small leaks are caught early
  • Stay on top of tankless descaling to avoid error codes and reduced flow
  • Set the water temperature around 120 degrees for safety and efficiency
  • Schedule a flush every year, or every six months if you have hard water
  • Ask about water treatment if flushing turns up heavy mineral buildup
  • Ask about the anode rod at each visit; it is the tank's main defense against rust
  • Do not store boxes, paint, or chemicals against the water heater
