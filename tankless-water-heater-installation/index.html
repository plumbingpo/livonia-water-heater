What makes tankless water heaters appealing? Mostly three things: a supply that does not run dry, lower standby energy use, and space. A tankless heater stays idle until you open a tap, then fires a high-output burner or electric element and heats the water in passing. With no stored water to keep warm, it wastes less energy between uses, and with regular descaling it can last two decades. It is not magic, though. Performance depends entirely on sizing and installation, which is where most of our work in Livonia, MI goes.

Sizing is where tankless installs succeed or fail. We size by flow rate and temperature rise: add up the fixtures you realistically run at the same time in gallons per minute, then factor in how cold the incoming water gets in winter, since a unit that delivers five gallons per minute at a mild rise may deliver noticeably less when the groundwater is cold. Then comes the infrastructure. A whole-home gas unit can draw 150,000 to 199,000 BTU, which often means a larger gas line or a meter check. Venting must match the unit, and electric tankless models can need well over 100 amps, sometimes enough to require a panel upgrade.

When we install a tankless heater, we build it to be serviced. That means isolation valves for descaling, a gas line with a sediment trap sized for the burner's full demand, venting that meets the manufacturer's rules for length and termination, and a condensate line with a neutralizer on condensing models. If you have hard water, we explain scale protection options before the unit goes on the wall. Optional recirculation can shorten the wait at distant fixtures. We handle permits where required, test the unit at full flow, and show you how it works. Call (734) 280-6296 from anywhere in Livonia for a demand assessment and a clear quote.

When to Call for Tankless Water Heater Installation

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • More people or more fixtures than your current heater was sized for
  • Showers turning lukewarm before everyone gets a turn
  • A tank water heater that is ten years old or more and due for replacement
  • Standby losses on a tank that sits idle for long stretches
  • Renovation work that makes gas line and vent changes easier to fit in
  • A remodel that would benefit from a compact wall-mounted heater
  • Interest in a longer-lasting heater with replaceable parts
  • An addition or guest suite that is a long run from the main heater

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Could a handy homeowner install tankless? Because the risks and the details stack up quickly. A burner that can draw nearly 199,000 BTU needs properly sized gas piping, and leaks or undersized lines are both dangerous and costly. Venting has to match the unit exactly, with the right material, slope, and termination clearances, or combustion gases can end up where they do not belong. Electric models may need a panel upgrade, and warranty terms often depend on a qualified installation. Permits and inspections are common requirements as well.

Our technicians handle the full picture. We size the unit by flow rate and winter temperature rise instead of guessing, confirm gas pressure with the burner at maximum, and install venting and condensate lines exactly to specification. Every install gets isolation valves for easy descaling and a properly piped relief valve. We also give honest advice about whether tankless suits your home at all. For some households, a high-efficiency tank or a heat pump water heater makes more sense, and you will hear that from us before you spend money on a system that does not match how you live.

How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here's where to begin:

  • Write down any error codes and call early rather than resetting repeatedly
  • In freezing weather, keep the unit powered and insulate exposed piping
  • Check the vent cap outside for nests, debris, or buildup
  • Set the temperature around 120F for safety and efficiency
  • Check and rinse the small filter screen at the cold inlet periodically
  • Hard water shortens tankless life, so ask about scale protection
  • Flush the unit with a descaling solution annually to keep scale off the heat exchanger
